re/read

Settings

Everything re/read does happens on this device (offline): translating, the dictionary, the reading list, your saved phrases. Nothing you read or select is ever sent anywhere. An internet connection is needed only when you want to download a new translation model or a dictionary - and only from the two URLs shown openly in those sections.

First steps

Nothing translates yet - the model that would do it is not yet on this device. Setting up takes a minute:

  1. Under "Translation models", find the language pair you need (the filter finds it fastest) and press "Download". The model is stored on this device and translates offline from then on - and the first model sets the language pair above by itself.
  2. Under "Dictionaries", download a dictionary for the same pair (optional, but worth it) - single words then get fuller answers than a translation alone, shown in the translation bubble below the translation.

Last, pin re/read to the toolbar: the puzzle-piece icon at the right end of the bar, then the gear next to re/read and "Pin to Toolbar". The button opens the reader, the reading list, your saved phrases and the switch for the site you are on.

Turns off machine translation (the model). The reader, the offline reading list and the highlighter keep working. With a language pair chosen, the bubble still shows dictionary meanings, and you can save phrases and see their underlines. Ideal for reading with dictionaries or in your native language. Your saved data is not deleted and returns when you turn this off.

With this on, selecting text on an ordinary page does nothing at all - open a page in the reader from re/read's button or with Alt+Shift+R. In the reader a selection is only a highlight for the moment: no bubble opens, a tap or Escape clears it, and Ctrl+C or Cmd+C copies it (on a touch screen nothing can be copied).

Limits the underlining of saved phrases and the translation bubble to the reader. Selecting text on an ordinary web page will only show an option to open the page in the reader. With it on, no web page can tell which of its words you have saved. On Android and iOS it is on by default.

Automatically saves every page you open in the reader for offline reading. Your saved pages, highlights and reading progress are kept safe even if the original website changes or disappears.

Shows only the translated text in the bubble. The action buttons (such as copy or save) appear only when you click or tap the bubble.

When you select text, the bubble shows right away what is otherwise shown only after a press on "More": the translated sentence and the dictionary entries. Worth keeping on with a dictionary installed - a translation model often gets a single word wrong, and the dictionary shows what it really means. The bubble over an already saved phrase shows only the saved translation - the sentence and the dictionary entries appear after you press "More".

A saved word is underlined in its other forms too: save "read", and "reads" and "reading" are underlined as well - the bubble over them shows what you saved for "read". Which forms count is decided by your dictionary for the language you read, so one has to be installed. For now only when you read English. Switched off, only the exact form you saved is underlined.

When you save a phrase from the bubble, the sentence around it is saved with it - the sentence as the page shows it, without its translation. It is shown under the phrase on the saved phrases page, folded to one line, and written into the third column of the export for Anki. For a phrase saved without a sentence, the sentence around it is saved the next time you open its bubble on a page. Like the phrase, the sentence is stored only on this device. Switched off, no new sentence is saved; the ones already saved are not deleted.

Translation models

Download a translation model here for each language pair you want to translate. These are official Mozilla files that run entirely offline on your device. A model translates sentences well, but a single word on its own is often translated wrongly - the result is cut short, or is a word that does not exist. For single words, a dictionary from the section below is the better source.

List from -

Add a model from files

To add by hand a model the list above does not offer, load its files here.

  • lex.50.50.enpl.s2t.bin
  • vocab.enpl.spm

Adding a model by hand makes no network request, and this page stays the way in on the day the place models are downloaded from stops answering.

Dictionaries

Add dictionaries to see detailed word definitions in the translation bubble. You can change the order in which they appear using the arrows.

The dictionaries listed below come from WikDict. You can also add your own - from files or from a link, and several for one language - in the sections under the list. Where to find them, each with its licence: reapps.eu/read.

    Add a dictionary from files

    Import your own dictionaries in the StarDict format. Unpack the archive, select the required files (.ifo, .idx, .dict), choose the languages and press Add dictionary.

    • name.ifo - what the dictionary calls itself
    • name.idx - the index of words
    • name.dict or name.dict.dz - the entries
    • name.syn - other spellings, when the dictionary has them

    A monolingual dictionary works too, for example English-English: choose the same language on both sides.

    Adding from files makes no network request. Any StarDict files work, including the catalogue's own, which are published at download.wikdict.com/dictionaries/stardict.

    Add a dictionary from a link

    Paste the address of a StarDict dictionary packed as a .zip file, choose the languages and press Download. The extension connects to that address once, when you press the button, and to nowhere else; nothing of yours is sent along. The archive is unpacked and checked on this device, exactly like files you pick yourself.

    Where to find dictionaries, monolingual ones too - English-English, for example - each with its licence: reapps.eu/read.

    Only https addresses are accepted. An archive that is not a .zip file (.tar.xz, .tar.zst) has to be unpacked and added from files.

    Switched-off sites

    A list of websites where the extension is switched off: no translation, no bubble, no underlines. Add a site with the switch under the extension's icon in the browser's toolbar while you are on it, or type its address below. There is no switch when both "Use without a translation model" and "No bubble when selecting text" are on: nothing happens on ordinary pages then, so there is nothing to switch off.

    The address as the browser shows it, for example example.org; www.example.org is the same site. A subdomain such as news.example.org is a separate site. A page's whole address can be pasted; only the site part is kept.

    Custom CSS

    For changing how the bubble, the reader page and the toolbar popup look with CSS rules of your own. This page stays out of their reach, so a wrong rule can always be undone here: clear the field and save.

    Example: .bubble { outline: 3px solid darkorange; } draws an orange line around every bubble.

    More about the CSS rules and the names

    The rules apply inside the translation bubble, on the reader page - its bar and lists included - and in the toolbar button's window, never on the pages you read. A wrong rule can break the look or the controls of those places until the field is cleared.

    Rules that would load anything from the network (url(), @import, @font-face) are refused. The names of the elements are kept from version to version where possible; when one has to change, the release notes say so. The names come from these files:

    tooltip.js (the bubble) reader.css (the reader) popup.css (the popup) page.css (shared by every page)
